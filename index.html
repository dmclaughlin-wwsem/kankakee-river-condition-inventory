Project Scope and Objectives

The 2025 inventory documented environmental conditions along approximately 74 miles of the Indiana Kankakee River. The project combines field observations, measurements, habitat information, laboratory samples, and immersive imagery to improve understanding of the river and make the information accessible to technical and public audiences.

The Kankakee River Explorer is intended to support environmental stewardship, education, planning, future monitoring, and continued exploration of the river corridor.

Study Methods

Field teams traveled the river by kayak over six survey days. The inventory incorporated timestamped GPS tracks, continuous water-quality sensor readings, laboratory nutrient samples, QHEI habitat assessments, verbal environmental observations, photographs and video, EarthViews 360° imagery, and other location-based information.

Indiana DNR river-mile reference coordinates provide a common spatial framework for relating information collected by different methods.

Partners and Funding

This Explorer presents work conducted as a project of the Pokagon Band of Potawatomi in cooperation with WaterWays|SEM, L3C. Funding was provided by the U.S. Environmental Protection Agency Great Lakes Restoration Initiative.

Additional survey partners, contributors, and acknowledgments can be added as the public project description is finalized.

Data Use and Interpretation

The Explorer combines information collected at different spatial and temporal resolutions. EarthViews previews, aerial flyovers, GPS tracks, river-mile references, and environmental measurements should be interpreted according to the notes provided in each dashboard.

The Explorer is a communication and discovery platform and does not replace original laboratory reports, agency records, or formal technical documents.
